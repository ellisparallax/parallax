# PARALLAX: THE RESIDUE FILES
## Series bible (one paragraph, taped inside the booth)
Two hosts. NOVA tells the story like your funniest friend who read the whole file. VECTOR is the analyst who loves NOVA but will absolutely stop the show to check a planet. Every cold open is a VERBATIM primary document. Every episode ends with a Verdict Card read aloud, confidence label and all, and one Residue Question the audience can actually chase. We are allowed to be funny about bureaucracy, acronyms, and ourselves. We are never funny about witnesses. Catchphrase out: "Stay skeptical, stay curious — and check your Venus."

# EPISODE 1 — "THE CORKSCREW OVER KAZAKHSTAN"
### (Case DOC-001 · 94 DUSHANBE 259 · Vault-verified verbatim)

**[COLD OPEN — single spot, paper rustle, NOVA reads slow and flat, like evidence]**

NOVA: "They watched the object for some forty minutes as it maneuvered in circles, corkscrews, and made ninety-degree turns at rapid rates of speed and under very high G's." ... "Comment: We have no opinion and report the above for what it may be worth."

VECTOR: That is a real State Department cable. Word for word.

NOVA: The United States government saw the word CORKSCREW in an official document and responded with the diplomatic equivalent of a shrug emoji.

VECTOR: "For what it may be worth." Sir, it was worth CIA distribution, but we'll get there.

**[THEME STING — 10 seconds, synth over shortwave crackle]**

NOVA: Welcome to Parallax: The Residue Files, the show where every story comes with receipts, and some of the receipts come with redactions. I'm Nova.

VECTOR: I'm Vector. Housekeeping promise, every episode: everything we quote tonight is from the primary record — a three-page unclassified cable, released in full, February 2026. We will tell you exactly where the document ends and our speculation begins. There will be a siren. I bought a little siren.

NOVA: He did. It's the size of a cupcake. Okay — ACT ONE. **The Night.** January 27th, 1994. A Boeing 747SP over western Kazakhstan, forty-one thousand feet, middle of the dark. And in the cockpit — I need you to picture this man — Captain Ed Rhodes. Chief pilot of Tajik Air. Formerly of Pan Am.

VECTOR: Which matters. Pan Am pilots are the retired rock stars of aviation. Decades of oceanic night flying. The cable literally records that he and his crew had seen — quote — "thousands of falling stars and other space junk."

NOVA: The man is a *meteor sommelier*. He's swirling the night sky going, mm, notes of Perseid. And out of the east comes a light — the cable says "enormous intensity" — coming over the horizon FAST, and way, WAY above them. And it starts... performing.

VECTOR: Circles. Corkscrews. Ninety-degree turns at speed. For forty minutes.

NOVA: Forty MINUTES. That's not a sighting, that's a *residency*. Meteors get eight seconds. This thing did a Vegas set.

VECTOR: And the description of the light is the detail I can't shake. They said it had a "bow wave" — like — direct quote — "a high-speed photo of a bullet in flight, in which a very small object gives off a much larger trailing wave of heat/light."

NOVA: Three professional pilots watching a bullet that changed its mind. Repeatedly. And Rhodes — because he is a certain generation of aviation man — reaches into his pocket and pulls out... an Olympus pocket camera.

VECTOR: The cable specifies the brand. The State Department wants history to know it was an Olympus.

NOVA: Hold that camera in your mind. That camera is the whole third act. **ACT TWO — The Record.** Because here's what makes this case my favorite kind of case: the story doesn't end when the light leaves. Forty-five minutes later, sun's coming up, and the 747 — doing over five hundred knots — flies UNDERNEATH the contrails the thing left behind.

VECTOR: They flew under the crime scene. There were skid marks. In the sky. Tracing the circles and corkscrews. And Rhodes estimates the altitude of those contrails at one hundred thousand feet — and then does the physics FOR us in the cable: there's too little air and moisture up there for ordinary propulsion to make contrails at all.

NOVA: So this isn't "guy sees light." This is: light performs impossible geometry for forty minutes, and then the sky itself keeps a *diary entry* about it, and three pilots physically drive under the diary.

VECTOR: Now — Nova. The siren. [tiny siren] Because before anyone gets carried away, we ran our own audit on this case. We rebuilt that exact sky. January 27th, 1994, 45 north, 55 east, pre-dawn.

NOVA: Tell them what you found, you monster.

VECTOR: Jupiter. Up. Twenty-eight degrees, south-southeast. A brilliant planet WAS in that sky, and Venus — history's number one impostor — was down. So: could a tired crew mistake Jupiter for something weird? It has happened to better crews.

NOVA: BUT.

VECTOR: But. Jupiter does not corkscrew. Jupiter does not do forty minutes of aerobatics and then adopt — cable's words — "a horizontal high-speed course" over the horizon. And Jupiter has never once left a contrail you can fly under. The planet was present and it is *insufficient*. That's the honest verdict: the easy answer was in the room, and the evidence walks right past it.

NOVA: And then the cable does the thing. The thing that made me put it down and stare at a wall. The routing block. Read the addressees.

VECTOR: From Embassy Dushanbe... to the Secretary of State... info Moscow, Tashkent, Ashgabat, Almaty, Beijing, Bishkek... CIA Washington DC, copy 0224... DIA Washington DC, copy 0232.

NOVA: The CIA and the Defense Intelligence Agency were CC'd. In 1994. On a UFO report from an airline pilot. Like it's a normal Tuesday. And the tag line — the State Department filed this under TSPA. Which means somewhere in Foggy Bottom there is a STANDING CATEGORY for "space, science... and whatever THAT was."

VECTOR: That's the real finding of Act Two, and it's bigger than one light: there was a quiet, functioning, routine channel for this material, decades before anyone said the letters A-A-R-O on television. The system always cared. It just cared *silently*.

NOVA: **ACT THREE — The Residue.** The camera, people. Remember the little Olympus? The cable says Rhodes took SEVERAL PHOTOS and would send copies to the Embassy, and to a named desk officer — Lowry Taylor, Tajikistan desk — quote, "if they come out."

VECTOR: And then... nothing. The photographs never appear in the record again. Thirty-two years. The cable surfaced. The photos didn't.

NOVA: That's our true-crime ending, except the missing body is FILM. And our whole archive has a pattern about this — it's the sharp evidence that goes missing. The blurry stuff? Flows like water. The clean stuff develops a... custody problem.

VECTOR: Verdict Card, read in full, as always. [paper unfold] Case: Kazakhstan 747, January 27, 1994. Document reliability: TIER ONE — verbatim State Department cable, released in full. Observation: TIER TWO — professional multi-witness. Prosaic candidates checked: seven planets — Jupiter present, insufficient; meteor — rejected by the witnesses' own expertise and forty-minute duration; satellite — no 1994 object performs powered corkscrews. Physical corroboration: persistent contrails at approximately one hundred thousand feet, physically overflown. Status: UNRESOLVED. Confidence in that word: high. And the Residue Question we leave you with —

NOVA: — WHERE ARE ED RHODES'S PHOTOGRAPHS? Our custody math says they're statistically overdue. There is a FOIA request with their name on it, the details are in the show notes, and if a listener shakes those photos loose, we will name an episode after you.

VECTOR: We report the above... for what it may be worth.

NOVA: Which, for the record, turned out to be a LOT. Stay skeptical, stay curious —

BOTH: — and check your Venus.
